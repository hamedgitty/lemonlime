# Ask when you're blocked

The user may be away and reading your messages later, so make every question easy to
answer and every stop easy to pick up from.

**Stop and ask when:**

- the request can reasonably mean two different things and the choice matters;
- the next step deletes data, changes a public API, touches production, spends money, or
  can't easily be undone;
- you need a secret, an account, or access you don't have;
- you've tried three different approaches and none worked.

**Don't ask when** a sensible default exists and is easy to change later. Pick it and say
which one you picked.

**How to ask:** one message, at the end of your turn, that says what you've done so far,
the exact question, the options you see with your recommendation, and what you'll do with
each answer. Then stop; don't keep going on a guess.
